the rules are and will be under --> /etc/udev/rules.d

They participate in a pipeline that starts when hardware appears and ends with userspace programs responding to that hardware.

We are not going to redirect the physical USB device somewhere else. We are going to observe the kernel's device events, apply host-side policy, and eventually hand the storage device to an isolated scanner.

udev can be loaded from multiple / several locations

some of those different locaion, serves a different purpose for the entire system 

Location	Purpose
/usr/lib/udev/rules.d/	--- Vendor and distribution-provided rules installed by packages
/etc/udev/rules.d/	--- Administrator-managed rules, where we should put our project-specific rule
/run/udev/rules.d/	--- Runtime rules, which may be generated or supplied during the current boot

We will not redirect the kernel event itself into Firecracker. Instead, we will build a controlled pipeline that uses the event to identify a device and then passes a carefully selected block device to a virtual machine.
